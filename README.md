# SemiBase census website

A static website about the 17,282 semigroups of orders one to six and their identity bases, published at
**<https://nasqret.github.io/semibase-site/>** and, from the same build, at
<https://bnaskrecki.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/semigroups6/>: one page per semigroup, per variety and per certified
basis, with search, a filterable list, an interactive graph of the varieties, and links to the Lean proofs in
[nasqret/semibase-order6](https://github.com/nasqret/semibase-order6), release 1.0.0, archived on Zenodo as
[doi:10.5281/zenodo.23109225](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23109225).

## What it shows

- `index.html`: the numbers, a search box, notable semigroups and how many variables the bases need.
- `s/<k>.html` for the class `[6, k]` and `s/<n>-<k>.html` for `[n, k]` with n at most five: a description, the
  Cayley table and its structure (idempotents, zero, identity, nilpotency, regularity, 𝒥-classes, rank,
  self-duality); the basis of its variety in the fewest variables, the basis with the fewest identities and the
  basis certified in Lean, or, for the four exceptions, the argument that no finite basis exists; the Lean theorem
  with a link to its line in the repository when it is public, the statement Lean checked, the method of the proof
  and the size of the code it needs; the variety it generates and the variety its transpose generates, the
  varieties directly above and below, the other semigroups that generate it; and the tables of the same order that
  differ from it in one or two entries.
- `v/<name>.html`, one per variety, counting a variety and its dual separately: `V[n, k]` is the variety generated
  by `[n, k]` and `V[n, k]ᵒᵖ` the one generated by its transposed table, named after the generator of least order,
  then least number (pages `v/<k>.html`, `v/<k>-op.html` for order six and `v/<n>-<k>.html`, `v/<n>-<k>-op.html`
  below). Each shows its bases in the fewest variables and with the fewest identities, with a lower bound on the
  number of variables and its witness, its certified bases, its dual, its name in the database of Araújo et al.
  when it has one, its neighbourhood in the inclusion order, its generators and its members of order at most six,
  transposes included. The pages `v/<k>.html` of the first release whose variety now has a new name redirect to it.
- `names.html`: the rule that names the varieties, fixed once and for all (see below), with its guarantees, the
  list of all 685 names, and a lookup that finds the semigroup and the name of the variety of any Cayley table of
  order at most six; `names-v1.json`, the frozen list of names. The four varieties without a finite basis have pages
  `v/3843.html`, `v/8564.html`, `v/8878.html` and `v/13747.html`.
- `b/<id>.html`, one per certified basis: all its identities and the classes it is certified for.
- `browse.html`: all classes in one table, with filters and sorting kept in the address.
- `varieties.html`: the 681 finitely based varieties as a sortable list, in the order of their names, and as an
  interactive graph of their inclusions. Selecting a variety colours everything above and below it; the view around
  a variety lays out only those, one row per step; search finds a variety or the variety of a semigroup; the address
  keeps the selection (`varieties.html#graph=5-540&around=2`), so a view can be shared. It works with a mouse, a
  keyboard and touch, and has a full-screen mode.
- `nonfinite.html`, `about.html`, and `build.json`, a record of the build: the commits it was made from and a hash
  of all its files, so that two copies of the site can be compared.

The site is plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript with relative links only, so it works from disk and from any
static web server, in any directory, on phones as well as on desktops.

## Names of varieties

Every variety generated by a semigroup of order at most six has a permanent name. `V[n,k]` is the variety
generated by the semigroup `[n,k]` = `SmallSemigroup(n,k)` of GAP Smallsemi (Cayley table
`MultiplicationTable(SmallSemigroup(n,k))`, row times column), and `V[n,k]^op` (shown `V[n, k]ᵒᵖ`) the variety
generated by its opposite, with the transposed table. A variety is named after the least triple `(n,k,o)` whose
semigroup generates it, triples compared by `n`, then `k`, then `o` (0 for the table, 1 for its transpose); this is
also the canonical order of the varieties. So a name never depends on bases, computations or hosting, `n` is the
least order of a generator, the dual of `V[n,k]` is `V[n,k]^op`, and extending the census to larger orders only
adds names. Version 1 (30 October 2026) has 685 names: 681 finitely based varieties and the four varieties of L, B₂¹,
A₂ᵍ and A₂¹, `V[6,3843]`, `V[6,8564]`, `V[6,8878]` and `V[6,13747]`. `names.html` states the rule, its guarantees
and the procedure to find the name of the variety of any finite semigroup.

`data/variety-names-v1.json` freezes version 1: every name in order with the triple and the table of its generator,
its dual and whether it is finitely based; for every class `n-k` the names of the varieties of its table and of its
transpose; and the names `V[k]` of the first release with the names that replace them. `prep/freeze_names.py` wrote it
once; `build.py` compares every name computed from the data with it and stops on any difference, and
`check_data.py` checks it by evaluation. **The file is never regenerated or edited**: a name is never changed, reused
or withdrawn. A later version, for example for semigroups of order seven, would add names in a new file
`variety-names-v2.json` that contains version 1 unchanged.

## Publishing, and keeping the two copies equal

Every push to `main` runs `.github/workflows/pages.yml`: it checks out this repository and the release of
semibase-order6 named in `LEAN_REF` (now `v1.0.0`), builds the site, runs both checks below, and publishes it on
GitHub Pages. To point the site at a later release, change `LEAN_REF`.

The build is deterministic: its date is the date of the commit of this repository, and nothing else in it depends
on the machine, so a build of the same commit against the same release is the same file by file. To keep the faculty
copy equal to GitHub Pages, `sync.sh` downloads the very artifact that the Pages workflow deployed for `HEAD` (with
the GitHub CLI), copies it to the faculty server with `rsync`, and compares `build.json` of both live copies:

```sh
./sync.sh            # after pushing to main, once the Pages workflow has succeeded
```

## Building

```sh
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 build.py        # about 25 seconds; writes site/
python3 check_data.py   # about a minute; checks the data and the names against the tables
python3 qa.py           # about 30 seconds; checks every page: links, HTML, wording
```

`build.py` needs Python 3.12 or later with numpy, and a checkout of semibase-order6 next to this directory (or set
`SEMIBASE_REPO`). It reads the catalogue of order six and the census map from that checkout, and the Lean links
point to its commit, so build from a commit that is on GitHub. `census.py` holds the data model that `build.py`,
`check_data.py` and the scripts in `prep/` share. The layout of the inclusion graph comes from Graphviz (`dot`); it
is kept in `data/graph-layout.json` with a hash of its input, so Graphviz is needed only when the varieties or
their order change. Open `site/index.html` to view the result.

Inputs, all in `data/`:

| File | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `sealed-bases-v1.json` | the certified basis of each finitely based class of order six, read back from Lean | bases-min, `data/order6-seal-20260924/` |
| `reduced-forms-v1.json` | each certified basis of order six without the identities that follow from the others (Vampire) | bases-min, same folder |
| `lower-classes-v1.json` | every class of order one to five: table, self-duality, certified basis, Lean theorem | `prep/prepare_lower.py`, from bases-min and the campaign |
| `rows-order6-bases.json`, `rows-order5-bases.json` | for each basis of order six and five, the tables of orders one to six and their transposes in which it holds | bases-min, `data/order6-lower-order-20261001/` |
| `rows-lower-bases-v1.json` | the same for the bases of orders one to four | `prep/prepare_lower.py` |
| `sgv-names-v1.json` | the names V(n,i) of the database of Araújo et al. and the tables of their generators | `prep/prepare_lower.py` |
| `variable-bounds-v1.json` | a lower bound on the number of variables of a basis of each variety, with a witness table | `prep/variable_bounds.py` |
| `few-variables-v1.json` | bases in fewer variables found by the search, and the attempts | `prep/fewvars.py`, `prep/collect_fewvars.py` |
| `countermodels-v1.json` | semigroups that satisfy every identity of a variety in k variables without lying in it | `prep/fewvars.py`, `prep/collect_refute.py` |
| `variety-names-v1.json` | the names of the varieties, version 1, frozen | `prep/freeze_names.py`, once |
| `graph-layout.json` | the Graphviz layout of the inclusion graph | made by `build.py` |

The table of order six comes from `research/order6/catalogue.json` of semibase-order6. The scripts in `prep/` read
sources that the build does not need (bases-min, the records of the campaign, the database of Araújo et al.) and
run once; `prep/fewvars.py` needs Vampire and ran on the WMI cluster. Their outputs are committed.

`check_data.py` evaluates identities in the tables, independently of Lean and Vampire: every finitely based class
and its transpose satisfy the certified basis; on 20,000 random pairs, a table lies in a variety by the rows exactly
when it satisfies the basis shown; no table of the four nonfinitely based classes lies in any variety; every basis
of the search holds in exactly the tables of its variety; every lower bound has a valid witness; the names of the
database belong to the varieties of their generators; and the names of the varieties: no two classes are isomorphic
or anti-isomorphic, every semigroup of order at most six generates the variety named for it (it satisfies the
certified basis of the generator of the name, and that generator satisfies its basis), the 681 finitely based
varieties are pairwise distinct, so each is named after its least generator, and three identities tell the four
varieties without a finite basis apart. `build.py` asserts the consistency of the inputs, including the frozen names,
and reports any theorem whose line it cannot find.

## What is proved where

The certified bases and the four nonfinite-basis results are theorems checked by Lean: for order six in the public
repository; for orders one to five partly there (1,064 of the 1,309 theorems, which the proofs of order six need)
and otherwise in the repository of the campaign, which is not public. The bases with the fewest identities and the
bases found by the search rest on Vampire, and the pages say so. The varieties and their inclusions follow from the
certified bases by evaluation, and so do the lower bounds on the number of variables. "Fewest" means the fewest
found; a basis in the fewest variables is proved optimal when its lower bound meets it, as the pages say.
